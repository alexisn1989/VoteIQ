## Donor-Legislative Correlation Analysis

### Key Findings by Legislator

#### High Fundraisers vs High Bill Introducers

**PARADOX: High Funding ≠ High Legislative Output**

| Legislator | Funding | Bills | Ratio |
|-----------|---------|-------|-------|
| Mark Sickles | $4.28M | 2 | 0.0005 (1 bill per $2.14M!) |
| Robert Deeds | $3.32M | 53 | 0.016 (1 bill per $62K) |
| David Marsden | $2.47M | 26 | 0.011 (1 bill per $95K) |

**Interpretation**: Sickles raises 2x more than Marsden but introduces 96% fewer bills. This suggests:
1. Other legislative roles (committee, leadership)
2. Different strategic approach (blocking vs proposing)
3. Possible disconnection between fundraising and bill work

#### Bill Introduction by Legislator

| Legislator | Bills | Funding | Bill Type |
|-----------|-------|---------|-----------|
| Deeds | 53 | $3.32M | Healthcare, education, government |
| Seibold | 35 | $771K | Diverse |
| McPike | 35 | $212K | Diverse |
| Hackworth | 34 | $598K | Diverse |
| Marsden | 26 | $2.47M | Diverse |
| Garrett | 24 | $557K | Diverse |
| Bennett-Parker | 20 | $1.01M | Diverse |
| Sickles | 2 | $4.28M | Unknown |
| Bulova | 11 | $109K | Diverse |

**Finding**: Funding and bill activity don't correlate. McPike ($212K) introduces as many bills as Seibold ($771K). Deeds is a clear outlier (53 bills on $3.32M).
