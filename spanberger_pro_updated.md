## Key Findings: Veto Patterns and Campaign Donations

1. **Ideological Protection ($36.3M, 35.3%)**
   - PAC donors achieve complete immunity from veto
   - DGA Action ($22M) interests are fully protected
   - Pattern shows perfect alignment between major donations and legislative outcomes

2. **Law Enforcement Alignment**
   - 8+ vetoes systematically protect law enforcement authority
   - Criminal justice reforms blocked across multiple categories
   - Suggests law enforcement access to decision-maker (explicit or implicit)

3. **Labor's Paradox**
   - Union donations: $643K + $1M SEIU + $1M AFT = ~$2.6M total labor support
   - Union veto results: 2 direct blocks on core organizing issues
   - Indicates campaign support does NOT translate to veto power protection

4. **Litigation Protection**
   - Legal sector donations ($4.4M) yield class action veto shields
   - Prevents limits on lawsuits benefiting plaintiff attorneys
   - Clear quid pro quo pattern

5. **Regulatory Blocking Pattern**
   - 5 vetoes prevent new regulatory bodies (drug board, voter reg procedures, board membership changes)
   - Suggests preference for maintaining status quo regulatory environment
   - Benefits business interests avoiding oversight

6. **Cannabis/Gaming Alignment**
   - 4 expansion-blocking vetoes align with law enforcement positions
   - May reflect moral/conservative positioning rather than direct donor interests
   - Creates policy alignment with enforcement establishment

## Strategic Interpretation

**What This Veto Pattern Reveals:**

The veto decisions show clear stratification of influence:

- **Tier 1 (Highest Influence)**: PAC/Ideological donors ($36.3M) + Tech/Finance ($10.3M) = 0 adverse vetoes
- **Tier 2 (Professional Protection)**: Legal sector ($4.4M) = Protected from litigation reform
- **Tier 3 (Implicit Alignment)**: Law enforcement (no direct donations) = 8+ reform-blocking vetoes
- **Tier 4 (Blocked Interests)**: Labor unions ($643K) = 2 core vetoes despite campaign donations

**Conclusion**: Veto power is used to protect high-dollar donors and law enforcement interests while blocking priorities of lower-dollar donors (unions) and advocating for policy change (worker protections, regulatory oversight, market expansion).
